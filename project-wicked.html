Web Design · 2024

Wicked
Landing Page

A landing page for the premier of wicked part 2.

  • Tools Figma, HTML, CSS
  • Format Webside
  • Role Research, UX, UI, Web build
the processthe process the processthe process the processthe process the processthe process
01

Research & inspiration

For this project, I looked at existing Wicked posters and stage productions to understand the visual style of the film. The branding uses strong contrasts between emerald green and soft pink, which represent Elphaba and Glinda.

02

Content research

For deciding what content to put on my landing page, I researched existing landing pages. These typically had a strong hero section with a title, short description and a main call to action, followed by supporting content. Based on my research, I organised my page into a hero, a red-carpet section as the focus, and then supporting sections like gallery and music.

03

Site structure

I mapped out the navigation and how each section would flow from the fixed nav, working out the hierarchy before touching the design.

04

Wireframes

I sketched eight wireframe layouts covering every section of the page — hero, red carpet, merch, formats, cast, acclaim, gallery and music — to establish the structure before styling.

Design direction: Emerald green and pink represent Elphaba and Glinda, with gold accents for a magical, celebratory feel. Cinzel for headings feels elegant and theatrical; Raleway keeps the body text readable and modern.

05

Colour & type

The palette was inspired directly by the Wicked characters and branding. Emerald green and pink represent Elphaba and Glinda, while gold accents add a magical, celebratory feel. A dark background helps the colours stand out and creates a more dramatic atmosphere.

06

Finished page

The final page brings together the hero, red-carpet screening, merch bundle, available formats, cast carousel, critical acclaim, gallery and music — all tied together by the dark green, emerald and pink palette.

Outcome

A single-page fan site for Wicked: For Good that leads with the red-carpet screening as the main call to action, then layers in merch, cast, gallery and music. The dark, theatrical palette keeps the film's drama front and centre while the layout stays easy to scan.
